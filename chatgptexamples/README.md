# Bone & Bloom — ChatGPT examples

Twelve flowers and twelve stylized insects, built from native TREE node graphs. Each includes an already-generated binary STL and three mesh renders: sculpture, alternate angle, and bone study. Open **index.html** to browse the collection.

## Open and edit

Load one JSON from **flowers/** or **insects/** in TREE. The only visible output initially is **FINAL · fused printable sculpture**. It builds once after loading. After changing a design, click that node's **Build now** button. Click **Export fused STL → Export** to download your edited result. The supplied STLs use the same native isosurface and exporter, with export scale **2**.

To see construction, turn FINAL's **show** off, then enable **Bone study · toggle show** or **Lofted skins · toggle show**. The bone view intentionally retains open body cages. The insect output path adds solid cores only for the fused print; it does not hide the hoops in the bone-study output.

## How the petals work

Each whorl starts with multiple petal spines. Each spine has fourteen joints by default. A child branch at each joint crosses the petal's full width. Its length follows a sine-power envelope of **pfrac**, so ribs widen through the middle and narrow again at the tip. Root offsets centre each rib on its spine; the remaining offsets cup its edges. Flower ribs extend in the spine's local Z direction, tangential to the blossom, while wing ribs extend in local X to give insects their fore/aft wing width.

Every Loft uses **span = whole set** and **group by = parent branch**. One node therefore produces a separate skin for every petal or wing spine. **Join Curves** collects these independent patches without stitching them together. The final isosurface joins material where the actual geometry touches.

The most useful controls are the spine node's **rads**, **length**, **angle**, and **spread**; **Spine curvature**; and the length / offset expression nodes. When changing petal width, change both the rib-length multiplier and matching centring-offset multiplier. Increase Loft **divide v** when extending a spine substantially, so the sampled membrane remains continuous. Keep **curve dir = rows** on FINAL.

## Insect construction

Each insect has separate head, thorax and abdomen axes; perpendicular closed joint-chain hoops defining their body skins; three pairs of jointed legs with coxae, knees, tapered tarsi and paired claws; two antennae; two eyes; and paired mouthparts. Species studies add elytra, membrane wings, antler teeth, a rostrum, a horn, raptorial spines, cerci or forceps. These are sculptural interpretations rather than anatomical reference models.

## Mesh checks and printing

All 24 graphs were serialized, reloaded, and executed through the actual TREE node implementations. Every loft produced patches. Each final isosurface was generated by TREE's native core at **224 cells along its longest axis**. Every supplied mesh has finite coordinates, positive signed volume, **one connected surface component**, and **exactly two incident triangles per mesh edge**. There are no detached anatomy fragments or enclosed secondary shells in the supplied files. Detailed results are in **validation/catalog.json**.

These checks establish closed connected geometry; they are not a physical print or a complete self-intersection / minimum-wall analysis. Review orientation and supports in your slicer, especially the thin feelers, petal edges and recurved tips. The models have no automatic support structures or flat bases. Dimensions below assume STL units are interpreted as millimetres. Export scale 2 gives nominal minimum skeleton diameter 1.68 mm before voxelization; measured local wall thickness can differ.

## Files

- **flowers/** and **insects/**: 24 editable native JSON graphs.
- **stl/**: 24 matching binary STL exports.
- **previews/**: 72 renders of actual mesh geometry, including open bone studies.
- Two contact sheets and an offline HTML gallery.
- **validation/catalog.json**: descriptions, bounds, mesh counts and per-Loft patch counts.
